Rodrigo Garro-Rivero

I am a PhD student in philosophy at University of Southern California (USC), co-chaired by Sam Clarke and John Hawthorne. I regularly attend Sami R. Yousif's Spatial Cognition Lab.

I work primarily at the intersection of philosophy of mind and cognitive science, with broader interests in philosophy of science and epistemology.

My current work is on representational formats. The same information can be stored as a PDF, a Word document, or a JPG, and such formats shape what we can do with it. Similarly, I study how formats shape what processes can do with representations in different formats. See more in Research.

Before coming to USC, I was a master's student at Virginia Tech (VT), and before that I did my BA at the Pontifical Catholic University of Peru (PUCP). I was born in Apurimac and raised in Cusco, Peru.

Research

My dissertation is on representational format: what individuates formats, and how representations in different formats interface with one another. I approach these questions largely, though not only, through cases of spatial representations. The projects belows develop these topics in more detail.

Publications

  1. Forthcoming BJPS

    Analogue Coordinates: A Hybrid Format for Representing Spatial Relations

    The British Journal for the Philosophy of Science

    Abstract

    Cognitive scientists and philosophers regularly appeal to representational formats to explain patterns of failure and success in psychological tasks. Often, this involves them analogizing mental representations to familiar forms of artefactual representations, such as sentences, pictures, graphs, maps, and so on. In this article, I suggest that this general strategy sometimes leads researchers to mistakenly attribute irrelevant properties of artefactual representations to our mental representations. I illustrate this point using the format of visuospatial representations as a case study. Recent evidence suggests that humans encode visuospatial relations in a polar coordinate format, comprising an independent pairing of angle and distance from an anchor point. I argue, however, that these mental coordinates differ markedly from familiar mathematical coordinates by comprising analogue rather than digital constituents. The result is a distinctive hybrid representational format that problematizes familiar ways of categorizing mental representations and illustrates how mental representations often contain overlooked combinations of properties.

    Cite

    Garro-Rivero, R. (in press). Analogue coordinates: A hybrid format for representing spatial relations. The British Journal for the Philosophy of Science. https://doi.org/10.1086/743817

Work in progress

  1. Under review In progress

    Place and Form: A Case Study in the Disunity of Core Knowledge

    With Sam Clarke and Sami R. Yousif

    We use the cases of the Place and Form systems to argue that core knowledge theory is not as unified as previously thought.

  2. Late stage In progress

    The Role of Translation in the Architecture of the Mind

    I provide a translation account of the interface problem.

  3. Early stage In progress

    Molyneux’s Question: Ways to Yes

    I argue that a positive answer to Molyneux’s question cannot rest on shared content alone: either the representations at issue have the same format, or a translation process converts between them.

  4. Early stage In progress

    Integrating Space: Toward a Transformationist Account of Egocentric and Allocentric Representations

    I argue that translating egocentric representations into allocentric representations requires an explicitly represented anchor, such as a reference direction or landmark.
